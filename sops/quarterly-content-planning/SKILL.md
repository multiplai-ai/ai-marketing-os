# Quarterly Content Planning SOP

Plan the next quarter of content from the current strategy before choosing individual article topics or filling a weekly calendar.

## Purpose

This SOP turns the content strategy into a focused three-month production plan. It is designed to keep content connected to perceptions and pillars while leaving room for timely ideas, channel learning, and realistic capacity.

The planning unit is:

```text
locked perceptions and pillars
  -> three monthly themes
  -> one anchor show / piece per month
  -> channel-specific distribution plan
  -> monthly calendars and briefs
```

This is a quarterly planning procedure, not a writing procedure. Do not draft the quarter's articles during this process unless a short outline is needed to test feasibility.

## Cadence and owner

- **Cadence:** Every three months; run during the last two weeks of the current quarter.
- **DRI:** The entity's content owner.
- **Approver:** The brand/operator owner.
- **Contributors:** Subject-matter experts, channel owners, designers, and distribution partners as needed.
- **Monthly follow-through:** Run the monthly content-calendar workflow in the last week of each preceding month.

## Required inputs

Resolve the operating repository and current strategy pointers from its instructions and applicable binding before starting.

Read these files in order:

1. `{brain}/strategy/content-strategy.md`
2. `{brain}/strategy/brand-strategy.md`, if present
3. `{brain}/strategy/positioning-strategy.md`, if present
4. `{brain}/strategy/conversion-pathway.md`, if present
5. `{brain}/content/production-log.md`, if present
6. The current quarter's calendar, campaign plans, and known business priorities
7. Relevant voice and channel playbooks

If the content strategy is missing or its perceptions/pillars are not approved, stop and route to the content-strategy workflow first. Do not invent a quarterly plan from topics alone.

## Outputs

By the end of the session, produce or update:

- A three-month theme map with pillar, perception, timeliness, and anchor piece for each month.
- A show and channel plan that distinguishes the recurring show from supporting distribution formats.
- A quarterly mileage map showing how each anchor becomes channel-specific assets.
- A production-capacity check and explicit exclusions.
- A short decision log entry in `{brain}/content/production-log.md` or the entity's approved planning log.
- Monthly planning handoffs for the content-calendar workflow.

The consumer's current approved strategy defines show names, show count, cadence,
channels, access levels and CTA policy. Core has no default newsletter lineup.

## Planning principles

1. **Perceptions before topics.** Every theme must reinforce at least one locked perception.
2. **Pillar breadth, perception depth.** Rotate across pillars while repeating the most important perceptions enough for the audience to remember them.
3. **Anchor first, derivatives second.** Choose the strongest monthly piece before filling social slots.
4. **One theme per month.** A theme is a focus lens, not a rigid promise; timely work can still enter when it serves the strategy.
5. **Distribution is planned before production.** Decide where the work will go and what each channel must do before drafting.
6. **Default to less.** Protect quality and distribution capacity. Do not add a show or asset merely to fill a quota.
7. **Repurpose with adaptation.** Reuse the underlying idea, but package it for the channel rather than copying the same asset everywhere.
8. **No dead ends.** Every substantial piece should point to a useful next step: another piece, a resource, a conversation, a subscription, or an appropriate offer.

## Quarterly workflow

### 1. Open the planning cycle

Record:

- Quarter being planned: `[Q# YYYY]`
- Planning date: `[YYYY-MM-DD]`
- Planning owner and approver
- Available production capacity
- Known launches, deadlines, events, customer work, or market moments
- Channels currently in scope

State the planning constraint in one sentence, for example: “One paid long-form article per month, promoted through LinkedIn and X, with no new recurring shows this quarter.”

### 2. Load and verify the strategy

Summarize the current strategy in a short table:

| Item | Current decision |
|---|---|
| Primary audience | `[audience]` |
| Primary perception | `[P# and statement]` |
| Supporting perceptions | `[P#s]` |
| Content pillars | `[list]` |
| Recurring shows | `[list]` |
| Primary channels | `[list]` |
| CTA posture | `[teach / subscribe / consult / other]` |
| Capacity constraint | `[constraint]` |

Check for drift:

- Are the perceptions still locked?
- Are the pillars still strategically useful, or merely topic buckets?
- Is the show portfolio still sustainable?
- Are channels still reaching the intended audience?
- Did the last quarter reveal a format, audience, or distribution insight that should change the plan?

If a foundational decision needs to change, record it separately as a strategy refresh. Do not quietly alter the quarterly plan to compensate.

### 3. Review the previous quarter

Use the production log and channel evidence to answer:

- Which pieces reinforced the intended perceptions most clearly?
- Which pillars received too much or too little attention?
- Which topics produced useful conversations, qualified engagement, subscriptions, or other intended outcomes?
- Which social formats performed best with the target audience on each channel?
- Which pieces were never properly distributed or repurposed?
- What consumed more capacity than expected?
- What should be repeated, improved, retired, or explicitly excluded?

Do not judge a piece as weak solely from initial performance if it received little distribution. Separate **content quality** from **distribution quality**.

Output a short “continue / change / stop” list.

### 4. Generate candidate themes

Generate 3–5 candidate themes for the quarter before choosing the final three. Score each candidate using the strategy rubric:

| Factor | Weight | Question |
|---|---:|---|
| Pillar rotation | 40% | Which important pillar has been underserved or needs a new angle? |
| Perception reinforcement | 35% | Which perception needs more repetition, client, or clarification? |
| Timeliness | 25% | What current market, customer, business, or cultural event makes this relevant now? |

Use available client, lived experience, customer language, and existing assets as tie-breakers. A theme should be specific enough to guide one month of work, but broad enough to support an anchor plus several derivatives.

Avoid:

- themes that are only keywords;
- themes that serve no perception;
- three months of the same pillar unless there is a deliberate campaign reason;
- themes that require a new show, channel, or production capability to work;
- themes chosen only because they sound interesting to the team.

### 5. Choose the three monthly themes

Select one theme per month and document it with this template:

```markdown
## [Month YYYY] — [Theme]

**Primary pillar:** [pillar]
**Secondary pillar(s):** [optional]
**Primary perception:** [P# and customer-belief statement]
**Supporting perception(s):** [P#s]
**Timeliness hook:** [why this quarter / month]
**Audience:** [specific role, segment, and mindset]
**Anchor show/piece:** [working title or problem]
**Business goal:** [awareness, authority, subscribers, qualified conversations, or other]
**client available:** [experience, examples, data, artifacts, customer language]
**Open question:** [what social or audience research should help answer]
```

Run the balance check:

- Each month has a clear primary pillar.
- The quarter covers the most important pillars without forcing equal rotation.
- Every month reinforces at least one perception.
- The primary perception appears often enough to compound.
- No more than 30% of planned pieces are directly product- or conversion-focused.
- The themes fit the actual production and distribution capacity.

Get approval on the three themes before moving into asset planning.

### 6. Confirm the show and social-format architecture

Do this after the themes are selected and before creating the monthly asset maps.

First confirm the recurring show(s):

| Show | Cadence | Job | Channel | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `[show]` | `[cadence]` | `[strategic job]` | `[channel]` | `[owner]` |

Then define the supporting distribution formats by channel:

| Channel | Job | Candidate formats | Selection rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `[channel]` | `[reach / conversation / conversion]` | `[formats]` | Keep formats that reach the right audience and support the theme |

Formats are a menu, not a quota. The quarterly plan should identify the formats available for each channel; the monthly calendar chooses the right ones for that month's anchor and audience signals.

Use the current strategy to distinguish named shows from supporting distribution formats. Do not infer a lineup from historical examples.

### 7. Build each month's anchor brief

Create a GACCS brief for each monthly anchor before drafting:

| Element | Required decision |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | What business or audience outcome should this piece create? |
| **Audience** | Which specific role, segment, and mindset is it for? |
| **Creative** | What is the working title, hook, format, core argument, and useful artifact? |
| **Channels** | Where is the free introduction, full piece, and promotion going? |
| **Stakeholders** | Who writes, reviews, contributes, designs, approves, and publishes? |

For each practical long-form anchor, also specify:

- the concrete operating problem;
- the diagnostic the reader can use;
- the framework or model;
- the practical fix, template, checklist, or workflow;
- implementation warnings;
- the next step and CTA;
- what portion is free versus paid, if gated.

### 8. Build the quarterly mileage map

For each anchor, map the planned distribution chain:

```text
approved anchor piece
  -> free newsletter introduction
  -> pre-publication thesis / diagnostic
  -> launch post
  -> adapted LinkedIn asset(s)
  -> adapted X asset(s)
  -> framework, checklist, or excerpt
  -> reader reaction / objection / follow-up
```

Do not require every derivative in every month. Mark each asset as **planned**, **optional**, or **only if the anchor earns it**. Include at least one post-publication redistribution or follow-up path so the anchor does not disappear after launch.

### 9. Run the capacity and distribution checks

Before approving the plan, verify:

| Check | Rule |
|---|---|
| New long-form anchors | One per month unless explicitly approved otherwise |
| New recurring shows | None by default; add only with capacity and strategy approval |
| New pieces per week | No more than five; fewer is preferred |
| Anchor derivatives | At least three useful derivatives or distribution moments per anchor |
| Redistribution | Include existing content where it is still strategically useful |
| Flex capacity | Preserve room for timely work and audience response |
| Product/conversion focus | Keep within the 30% guideline |
| Channel fit | Each asset has a real channel job and audience rationale |

If a check fails, reduce scope before adding people, channels, or frequency.

### 10. Create the monthly handoffs

Create one handoff per month for the monthly content-calendar workflow. Each handoff should include:

- month and approved theme;
- pillar and perception;
- anchor GACCS brief;
- planned social-format menu;
- channel and CTA rules;
- target publication window;
- known contributors and review deadlines;
- optional and flex assets;
- links to relevant client, prior content, and audience research.

The monthly calendar then turns this handoff into a four-week schedule. It may adjust sequencing for timeliness, but it should not change the approved theme, show architecture, or strategic audience without flagging the change.

### 11. Record decisions and close the cycle

Append a short entry to the approved planning log:

```markdown
## Quarterly Content Plan — [Q# YYYY]

- **Planning date:** [YYYY-MM-DD]
- **Themes:** [Month 1]; [Month 2]; [Month 3]
- **Shows:** [approved recurring shows]
- **Primary channels:** [channels]
- **Social-format decision:** [approved menu / formats to test / formats retired]
- **Capacity constraint:** [constraint]
- **Major exclusions:** [what will not be produced]
- **Next handoff:** [date or file]
```

Confirm that the source-of-truth strategy, planning log, and monthly handoffs point to the same decisions. Do not leave the quarter's plan only in chat or Slack.

## Approval gate

The quarter is ready for monthly execution only when the approver confirms:

- [ ] The three themes are approved.
- [ ] Each theme maps to a pillar and perception.
- [ ] Each month has one approved anchor.
- [ ] The recurring show portfolio is explicit.
- [ ] Primary channels and channel jobs are explicit.
- [ ] Social formats are a bounded menu, not an unbounded list.
- [ ] Each anchor has a GACCS brief and mileage map.
- [ ] Capacity, distribution, and 30% juice checks pass.
- [ ] Exclusions and flex capacity are recorded.
- [ ] Monthly handoffs are ready.
