# GEO method and measurement limits

This is the operating guide for the AI Marketing OS GEO suite. It replaces
runtime references to the excluded third-party framework corpus. The procedures
and tools remain in their named workflow folders; no private Core checkout is
required.

## Workflow sequence (formerly Framework 01)

1. Agree the offer, audience, market, competitors and business question.
2. Build a fixed, versioned prompt set. Distinguish unaided category questions
   from questions naming a vendor; report them separately when comparing brands.
3. Measure a dated baseline using explicit API models or supplied exports.
4. Audit priority URLs for access, evidence, clear answers and content quality.
5. Inspect cited domains to distinguish owned-page fixes from earned coverage.
6. Restructure selected pages while preserving facts, voice, links and claims.
7. Prioritize changes with owners, dependencies and acceptance criteria. Repeat
   the same measurement after changes; document model and prompt-set changes.

## Content heuristics (formerly Framework 02)

A reader should be able to find the page's answer early, understand each section
in context, and verify meaningful claims. Use clear definitions, useful headings,
specific entities and source links. Include comparison tables only when the
source supports the comparison. Keep content useful to humans first.

The audit's five scores are heuristics, not validated predictions of citations.
The early-answer, heading, entity, factual-detail and readability checks can
misread unusual layouts or specialist writing. Inspect the underlying page and
report limitations. Question-heading percentages and a 15-point score gain are
review targets, never reasons to invent facts, flatten voice, or change claims.

## Measurement rules (formerly Framework 03)

Keep prompts, brands/aliases, models, sampling count and dates with every run.
Share of answers is cited responses divided by successful sampled responses;
report failures separately. Distinguish mentions from linked citations and
primary recommendations from secondary mentions. A positional classifier is a
heuristic: manually review a sample, especially ambiguous aliases and negation.
Report a paid classifier's errors or fallback rather than hiding them.

Shopping-intent answer share is a visibility metric, not pipeline attribution.
Repeated answers from one model may be correlated. Wilson intervals describe
the sample under their assumptions; they do not establish market-wide coverage.
Adaptive stopping and small samples limit comparability. Revenue calculations
are explicitly labeled scenarios with traceable assumptions, not forecasts.

## Search-quality guardrails (formerly Framework 04)

Preserve canonical URLs, internal links, structured data, factual caveats and
locked sections. Reapply and verify metadata in the CMS after a Markdown edit.
Use the labels SEO+/GEO+, GEO+ only, and GEO+ but SEO- as internal hypotheses
about benefits and tradeoffs, not proven rankings. Explain the expected mechanism
and how it will be measured. Avoid mass low-value pages, false freshness,
fabricated comparisons, hidden instructions to AI systems and unsupported claims.

Publish nothing during measurement or restructuring. Return drafts and a change
log for review. Resolve missing inputs and tool failures visibly; do not replace
the procedure with an improvised generic answer.
