# Positioning recommendation

Sources read: `examples/strategy-evaluation/inputs/normal.md` S1–S4 and `examples/strategy-evaluation/outputs/normal/discovery-intake.md`. Destination: `examples/strategy-evaluation/outputs/normal/positioning-strategy.md`. All input is fictional; no external research was performed.

Recommend positioning Fieldnote Desk against collecting job attachments through text threads and a spreadsheet. That is the actual alternative reported in S1 and recognizable in the three S2 interviews. Lead with seeing what is missing for each job before owner review. The founder has not adopted this anchor; it is a draft choice that can carry the rest of this analysis without pretending approval.

## Anchor and differentiation

S2 B says, “I need to see what’s missing without opening every text thread.” The specific problem is the coordinator's attachment chase, not a vague promise of business growth. Fieldnote Desk provides a shared checklist and missing-evidence view associated with job references; responses can be attached to the correct job (S1). Proposed contrast: a job-based view of missing attachments versus searching conversations to reconstruct the packet.

| Proposed advantage | Support | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Inspect what is missing for each job | S1: shared checklists and missing-evidence view | Product capability, not measured time saved |
| Keep links associated with job references | S1: CSV job import and manually pasted links | No accounting integration; not automatic ingestion |
| Prepare the packet for the owner's review | S1 workflow | Not inspection certification, report creation or invoice sending |

S3 describes one self-reported 70-minute collection session and a different 45-minute session during a pilot, each involving 12 jobs in different weeks. Do not use that as a percentage-saving claim, typical outcome, causal effect or evidence of faster payment. No comparative product benchmark exists. S2 quotes are internal research language without public permission.

## Segment recommendation

Stay with independent residential inspection firms with 2–8 inspectors, specifically those where a coordinator collects attachments before owner review. Six paid firms and the current workflow are supplied evidence (S1); they do not establish the whole segment's willingness to buy. Multiple-segment scoring is unnecessary here. Working scorecard: problem evidence is promising but limited to three interviews; differentiated workflow fit is plausible; access is constrained despite six founder referrals; market size is unmeasured. Do not pursue large enterprise segments merely to inflate opportunity.

## Draft narrative

One sentence: **Fieldnote Desk helps inspection offices see which job photos and links are still missing before owner review.**

Elevator pitch: Fieldnote Desk gives small inspection offices a shared checklist for collecting job photos and file links. Instead of reconstructing a packet from text threads and a spreadsheet, a coordinator can see what is missing and attach responses to the right job.

Homepage direction:

- Headline: **See what’s missing before the owner reviews the job.**
- Subhead: Collect inspection photos and file links in a job-based checklist for your office and inspectors.
- Problem: When attachments live across text conversations, preparing a job packet becomes a search task.
- Solution: Import job references, add file links and use the missing-evidence view to prepare the packet for review.
- Supporting points: identify missing attachments; associate responses with the right job; keep the owner review handoff visible.
- Proof structure: show the existing synthetic-job screenshot and walkthrough after checking that each depicted capability matches S1. Reserve quotes until permission is obtained. No customer logos or performance promises.
- Proposed action: request a demo. Booking destination is unresolved; no invented URL.

## Decisions and validation

Owner review needed: primary anchor and main promise; whether coordinator-led interest is the intended sales entry point. Then test the draft explanation with a few target coordinators and owners: have them describe what it does without prompting, name their current collection method, and identify a reason they would or would not pay $120/month. These are proposed interviews, not performed research.

Carry forward the 90-day goal of four new paid firms, two-per-month onboarding ceiling and four weekly marketing hours. The narrative should support that focused effort, not imply predicted conversions.
