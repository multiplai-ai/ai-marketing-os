## Fuel and distribution diagnosis

The founder has useful starting assets, not a demonstrated publishing system: a five-slide walkthrough, synthetic screenshot, blank checklist and internal-only interview language (S4). The 180 LinkedIn connections have unknown relevance; the 24 permissioned prior-demo contacts offer a small addressable list, not proven channel performance. Recommendation: start small, test relevance, and keep creation tied to specific workflow questions. Tier 1 for each twice-monthly anchor: a concise version for the existing permissioned newsletter list and one adapted LinkedIn post, after approval. Tier 2: selectively reuse the strongest checklist or demonstration when a relevant conversation arises; no community access or partnerships are assumed. Tier 3: none for now; paid ads are excluded and no external outreach is authorized in this exercise.

## Proposed recurring show

Working title: **Before Owner Review**. One practical 400–600-word note every other week, written and reviewed by the founder. Each note has one job-packet problem, one practical step, and one next action. Day/time and publishing software remain unspecified. LinkedIn receives a short adaptation, not a separate research project. No video program is proposed because no editor exists and the founder's time is constrained.

Four-week example: two notes and two adapted posts. Three of these four assets educate on packet preparation or handoff; one can show the product and invite a demo. That 25% direct-product mix is an initial editorial choice, not a policy or promise of performance. The next action on an educational note can be using the blank checklist or replying with a workflow question; it need not always be a sales pitch.

## Theme framework and first cycle

Proposed first three monthly themes, to be dated after the owner selects the start month:

1. **Find the missing attachments.** Anchor: a checklist for identifying what is absent from a job packet. Reuse the blank checklist; distribute a short note and adaptation. Next action: try the checklist on one job. Owner: founder. Review: ensure steps are relevant, not inspection/compliance advice.
2. **Make the handoff visible.** Anchor: the coordinator–inspector–owner sequence with one synthetic example. Use a source-checked excerpt of the walkthrough after inspecting it. Next action: compare the steps with the reader's actual process. Owner/reviewer: founder, with optional advisor input inside the time budget.
3. **Evaluate a shared checklist.** Anchor: product fit and manual-input limits using a synthetic job. Next action: request a demo through an owner-selected destination; no fabricated link. Owner/reviewer: founder. Check available onboarding slots before offering start dates.

These themes are an initial framework, not three approved monthly production briefs. There is no previous quarter or event evidence, so neither rotation history nor timeliness scores can be computed. In later selection, consider neglected relevant pillars, which perception needs support and genuine events; do not invent seasonality to fill a weighted rubric.

## Editorial principles

1. Start with one task the coordinator recognizes; avoid generalized transformation claims.
2. Explain manual steps honestly: CSV references, pasted links and drafted reminders are not automated integrations.
3. Use synthetic demonstrations until real case material and permissions are obtained.
4. Treat the S3 70/45-minute two-week observation as an internal research lead, not a universal saving or causal result.
5. Keep public quotes and logos out until permission exists; S2 currently informs language internally.
6. Give each piece a useful next step, including non-sales actions.
7. Reduce scope when the four-hour budget is exceeded; protect review time.
8. Record what happened before declaring a channel or message effective.
