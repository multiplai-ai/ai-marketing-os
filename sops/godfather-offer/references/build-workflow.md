# Guided build

Keep the user in conversation; these stages are an internal guide, not a form to
paste. Ask the highest-value unanswered question, reflect a short synthesis and
move on. Encourage dictation once at the start; later offer it only when the user
seems stuck. “Tell me about the last real client you won” is easier to answer
than a marketing taxonomy. Do not force a timing promise.

1. **Buyer and buying moment.** Resolve one buyer, urgent recognized problem,
   decision-maker, alternatives and real buyer language. Connect research to the
   user's experience; identify where they disagree. Separate expensive business
   problems from attractive but unsupported messaging ideas.
2. **Outcome and mechanism.** Identify the desired business outcome, a bounded
   commitment the provider controls, how the work creates value, first useful
   milestone, delivery time, client effort and relevant proof. Ask what successful
   acceptance looks like. A sample proves work quality, not revenue impact.
3. **Choose the offer.** Develop 2–3 meaningfully different candidate structures
   where plausible, explain your recommendation, and let the user choose. A
   project delivers a finite change; advisory supports recurring decisions;
   execution delivers genuinely recurring work. Do not force all three or a
   retainer when there is no recurring need. A paid diagnostic has standalone
   value even if the buyer never purchases implementation.
4. **Scope, terms and economics.** Define inclusions, exclusions, acceptance,
   dependencies and change rules. For retainers add capacity/queue, cadence,
   response windows, setup, renewal value and cancellation. Ask for proposed
   fee/currency and realistic delivery costs. Keep proposed terms distinct from
   approved policy and confirm ambiguous dictated figures.
5. **Risk and reasons to act.** Identify buyer risk and offer two feasible ways
   to reduce it. If a guarantee fits, specify trigger, remedy, eligibility,
   claim window and maximum exposure. Do not promise uncontrollable revenue.
   Add bonuses only when they remove a real obstacle. Use an honest rationale;
   urgency/scarcity must have a documented capacity/date basis or be omitted.
6. **Write and challenge.** Draft a concise offer statement and direct-response
   flow. Resolve author, language and channel; do not invent a brand voice.
   Critique as buyer and delivery lead: why this, why now, why us, what effort,
   what risk, and what happens if it fails? Ask only questions that materially
   affect the offer; revise using the user's answers.
7. **Pilot and deliver.** Propose a bounded test of paid demand, client fit and
   delivery contribution, with owner-set audience/channel/budget/time/stop rule.
   Retainers also track actual workload and renewal. Mark missing parameters as
   decisions. Deliver the document; do not launch anything.

## Arithmetic

Calculate from user-supplied nonnegative finite numbers. Missing is unknown,
not zero. Zero fee makes margin undefined. A target margin must be >=0 and <100%.
State currency and period; never mix project totals and monthly costs.

- Delivery cost = hours × loaded hourly cost + other delivery costs.
- Delivery contribution = fee − delivery cost.
- Delivery margin = contribution ÷ fee.
- Target-margin floor = delivery cost ÷ (1 − target margin as a decimal).
- Downside contribution = contribution − cash remedy − extra rework cost.
- Retainer setup contribution = one-time setup fee − one-time setup cost.

Avoid double-counting rework included in delivery cost or remedies that cannot
occur together. Describe the downside scenario. These figures exclude acquisition,
general overhead and tax. The cost floor is not a selling price. Use available
calculation/code tools and reconcile results; do not claim net profit or buyer
ROI. If costs are unknown, deliver the offer with an explicit economics gap.

## Session continuity

Track business/buyer/market, research receipt, stage, user decisions, selected
structure, scope/terms, numbers with units, proof, copy, unresolved questions and
next step. Retain in conversation or host session state; do not promise automatic
cross-chat persistence. On resume read supplied prior brief, avoid repeat questions,
and refresh only affected research. User corrections supersede prior assumptions.
