# Content purpose

Use the supplied brief to choose the reader's job before polishing the hook.
Growth earns attention from relevant new readers; Authority demonstrates useful
judgment; Conversion offers an available next step; Personal reveals an actual
experience or value. One primary purpose can have secondary effects. The monthly
planner owns proportions; the writer never adds quota filler to a batch.

Explain at the depth the intended buyer needs. For a senior buyer, translate
technical choices into work, decisions, costs, risks, or outcomes they understand.
For a technical buyer, precise implementation detail may be essential. Avoid
unexplained jargon at the opening; do not assume all buyers dislike technical work.

A sharp opinion needs a reason and an implication, not manufactured controversy.
News or recognizable names can supply context but are not evidence of reach.
Verify the actual event/quote before responding to it. Case studies need real
source-backed outcomes and the right usage permission. Personal writing needs
the author's actual experience; do not turn a third party's story into theirs.

Open with a concrete tension, observation, or consequence that the body resolves.
Build one coherent point with supported examples, then land the implication.
Use paragraphing and formats that fit the author's voice; questions, fragments,
short lines, humor, and CTAs are choices rather than a mandatory house style.
Vary the argument and evidence across a batch, not just its first line.

For a conversion goal, check the actual destination and the promised action.
No usable destination or confirmed offer means the CTA remains a proposed line
with a visible hold, or use a supported alternative the user accepts. Never
invent availability, a testimonial, price, or limited capacity. A soft CTA in an
authority post does not automatically turn it into primary Conversion content.
Keep any rationale and unresolved claims outside the clean post copy.
