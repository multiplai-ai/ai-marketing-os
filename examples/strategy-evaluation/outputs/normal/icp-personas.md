# Workflow-based ICP

Read: `examples/strategy-evaluation/inputs/normal.md` S1–S4; `examples/strategy-evaluation/outputs/normal/discovery-intake.md`; `examples/strategy-evaluation/outputs/normal/positioning-strategy.md`. Output: `examples/strategy-evaluation/outputs/normal/icp-personas.md`. These are fictional inputs and offline recommendations.

**Recommended ICP:** office coordinators at independent residential inspection firms with 2–8 inspectors who collect missing job photos and links through text threads and a spreadsheet before the owner reviews the packet. S1 reports this workflow and firm size; the positioning artifact recommends the text/spreadsheet anchor provisionally. Marketing should first make the coordinator's task recognizable, then give the owner a concrete evaluation of the workflow and $120 monthly price. This entry-point recommendation has not been tested or approved.

## Workflow and fit

The trigger is completed site work with missing attachments. The coordinator requests links, associates responses with jobs, checks what is missing and hands ready packets to the owner; inspectors supply links. It commonly occurs Friday in S1, not necessarily every firm's universal schedule. This is a narrow collection-and-handoff workflow inside inspection operations, not full inspection management. The consequences beyond inconvenience and the owner's inability to review missing material are unmeasured; do not claim faster invoicing or revenue lift.

Qualification: ask a prospect to walk through the most recent packet. Positive signals to test are repeated missing-attachment searches, a coordinator who owns collection, an owner willing to review a sample packet, and compatibility with CSV/manual-link input. Exclude firms that do not do this workflow, require automatic reminders/accounting integration as a purchase condition, or need certified inspection reporting rather than attachment collection. A solo inspector may do this work, but falls outside the current stated 2–8-inspector target and is not automatically the next segment. Geography and firm revenue were not supplied and are not targeting filters.

The two quotes above are coordinator statements, including a coordinator's account of inspector reluctance; neither is an inspector interview. They lack public usage permission. No age, income, morning routine, personal ambition, favorite podcast or formal reporting line is known. Trust sources are also unknown. Founder referrals show a past acquisition route, not a general preference of every persona.

Proposed coordinator hook: “See which photos and links are still missing for each job.” Proposed owner hook: “Review the job packet with its missing attachments visible.” These are draft copy, not quotations. Demonstrate actual checklist behavior rather than promising saved time. Address the inspector concern with a walkthrough and observed usability, not an unsupported “zero training” claim.

| Decision → adoption | First packet assembled | Observe coordinator/inspector handoff | Packet completion experience and retained use; no baseline yet |

These are proposed transitions, not observed funnel data. LinkedIn and the 24-contact permissioned list can carry selected content after approval, but channel fit and conversion are unknown. Do not invent a booking URL.

## Research plan and handoff

Start with a small set of coordinator–owner pairs where access already exists, within the founder's four weekly marketing hours. The advisor's 20 minutes every other week can refine questions, not substitute for market validation. Ask: “Walk me through your last incomplete packet”; “What did you use and what got in the way?”; “Who would decide whether to change it?”; “What would make a shared checklist unsuitable?” Have an inspector demonstrate their part rather than relying solely on coordinator perceptions. Record role, source date, exact relevant phrases and permission status. This is a proposed plan; no interviews were conducted.

Keep the positioning anchor provisional. Brand strategy should use these role distinctions, avoid imagined buyer psychology, and map claims to S1 capabilities/S2 concerns. S3's two-week pilot observation remains uncontrolled and cannot support a universal outcome. Adoption, quote permissions, channel choice and conversion expectations remain open; the four-new-firm goal and two-per-month onboarding limit remain constraints rather than a forecast.
