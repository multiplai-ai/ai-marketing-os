# Evaluate against the video's tests

Use this after drafting and after visual composition. The video supplies qualitative checks and test priorities, not a validated numeric scorecard. The statuses below are our implementation: **pass**, **revise**, **blocked by evidence/input**, **runtime untested**, or **not applicable with reason**. Do not average critical failures into a passing percentage.

For each check record the exact copy/node reviewed, observed evidence, finding and correction. Repeat affected checks after changes. An independent reviewer is useful where available and authorized; otherwise disclose self-review. Never label a model review as owner approval or market validation.

| Video-derived test | Evaluation question / failure condition |
| --- | --- |
| Market recognition · 00:31–04:38 | Can the argument be traced to a specific buyer situation and language? Generic growth or beautiful-work claims without a recognizable situation need revision. |
| Awareness fit · 07:45–11:22 | Does a cold stranger receive the context needed to understand the problem and next step? A page assuming brand familiarity fails for cold traffic. |
| Spoken-pitch test · 11:22–16:02 | Read the page aloud. Does it sound like an informed person persuading one buyer, or a formal list of agency capabilities? Without owner pitch evidence, mark the source limitation. |
| First-screen comprehension · 23:21–30:35 | On a phone, can the reader identify who it is for, the wanted outcome, why to continue and the next action? An oversized headline that hides the offer/CTA needs revision. |
| Clear headline and curiosity · 25:56–27:30 | Is the headline instantly understandable and tied to a desired outcome? Does the subhead add specificity and a reason to read, instead of explaining the entire service? Only use verified timeframes or guarantees. |
| Consumption path · 27:30–30:04 | Does the page make the pitch easy to consume, with a relevant video entry and benefit-led action? Missing video is a production gap. A separate VSL page is evaluated as a user-requested adaptation, not silently counted as the video's default. |
| Visual earning / readability · 30:35–32:40 | For every image ask what it helps the visitor understand or believe. Remove filler. Check paragraph length, contrast and at least 14px body copy at real mobile size. |
| Headings-only story · 33:12–35:15 | Read only the headline and section headings in order. Can you follow problem, solution, difference, proof, offer and next action? Titles such as “Our process” without a selling point fail this test. |
| Claim–receipt test · 35:47–37:52 | Highlight every factual promise. Can the adjacent evidence substantiate that exact claim? A design sample proves design capability, not bookings. Delete unsupported claims and record missing proof as a weakness. |
| Why this works differently · 38:22–41:25 | Can the reader explain the problem mechanism, solution mechanism and named approach? Naming ordinary steps without explaining the causal difference fails. |
| Food-court offer test · 41:55–45:32 | Say the offer in one sentence to a relevant cold prospect. Is there an obvious reason to raise a hand besides being sold to? Write what they get even if they decline the paid service. If that answer is vague, revise the offer before redesigning the page. This is a qualitative review, not a measured response rate. |
| Value / risk / extras · 46:03–50:11 | Is the actual value specific and the next commitment clear? Are applicable risk terms credible? Meaningless extras and fabricated anchors fail; absent approved terms stay explicitly pending. |
| Buyer-objection coverage · 50:11–52:45 | Are the questions grounded in actual buyers and ordered by known purchase importance? Inferred questions cannot be called customer-validated. |
| Real activity · 53:47–54:47 | Is each activity message tied to a real event? No data means no popup, not a fabricated demo that looks live. |
| Speed · 55:18–57:55 | Check real mobile performance and media cost. A design screenshot cannot establish loading performance, form delivery or video playback. |
| Commercial validation · 59:28–01:04:04 | Are planned tests ordered by angle, headline/subhead and offer? Can leads be connected to qualified meetings and customers? Conversion rate alone cannot establish the winning page. |

## Decision

Distinguish four conclusions: copy/design fit; missing proof or offer decisions; technical launch readiness; market validation. Do not call the result “high converting” because it passes a design review. Report the strongest remaining weakness and the concrete next action. Fix solvable copy/design defects before handing off; don't use this record as an excuse to leave avoidable blandness in place.
