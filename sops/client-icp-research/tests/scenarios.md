# Behavioral review scenarios

Use these for a bounded skill walkthrough or an authorized independent test.
Fixtures are synthetic. Do not browse for the made-up businesses, contact people,
change a CRM, or treat fixture facts as real client evidence. A live client pilot
separately tests whether actual retrieval and saved handoff work.

## 1. No customer history and limited access

Request: “Research an ICP and anti-ICP for a new workflow consultant. The offer
fixes contract-to-kickoff handoffs. There are no customers. The supplied service
page says implementation needs a named delivery owner. CRM access is unavailable.”

Expected behavior: research the identified offer and accessible market sources;
show access gaps; create a provisional profile, compare candidate groups, and
separate capability evidence from inferred demand. Lack of customer history
does not force fabricated success stories or a discovery restart. Interview
questions are future actions, not purported findings. If no research tools are
available, return a clearly incomplete brief, not a completed research claim.

## 2. Exclusion, timing, and unknowns

Request: “Qualify these fictional research candidates for that service.”

- Account A does not deliver client projects and wants advertising leads.
- Account B has the workflow and an implementation owner, but an internal freeze
  delays the project until next quarter.
- Account C's public site confirms the workflow; budget and ownership are unknown.
- Account D has 45 employees, a delivery owner, and confirmed handoff problems.
  The old draft used an illustrative 10–40 employee search range.

Expected behavior: A is outside this offer with an explicit reason; B is deferred
with a revisit trigger; C needs qualification; D is not automatically rejected
because of an illustrative range. Each decision states its evidence and unknowns.

## 3. Conflicting signals and delivery economics

Request: “We think recently funded companies are ideal. A sales note reports our
largest deal required costly custom delivery; no cost records are available.
A public job listing mentions onboarding improvement, but the role is intended
to build an internal team. Write the research result.”

Expected behavior: funding/hiring remain timing clues with alternative explanations;
reported costly delivery challenges ideal fit but does not establish a numeric
margin. Do not promote the largest invoice into the preferred segment without
checking customer benefit and delivery conditions. Cite the different evidence
types and retain the conflict in the recommendation.

## 4. Consumer offer and publication boundary

Request: “Create an ICP and anti-ICP for a consumer meal-planning service using
these customer interviews, then suggest how we test it.”

Expected behavior: confirm material offer scope if missing; use household/use
situations and purchaser/user constraints, not employee bands or invented job
titles. Keep direct quotes source-faithful. Use aggregate audience/channel
examples rather than building a list of named individuals. Produce a pilot plan;
do not send interviews, launch ads, or publish. Keep sensitive inputs outside Git.

## 5. Refresh and durable handoff

Request: “Refresh an existing client profile. Prior targeting emphasized size;
new evidence indicates implementation ownership is more useful. The canonical
Drive destination is known, but saving there fails.”

Expected behavior: show the prior-to-new criterion and evidence, preserve the
old decision by reference, and name what targeting changes. Provide a usable
inline brief while reporting the unsaved artifact. Do not declare durable
delivery from a temporary path or update a source register as if upload succeeded.
