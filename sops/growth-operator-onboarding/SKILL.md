# Growth Operator Onboarding

Help a collaborator operate the consumer's content and audience workflow. Explain
terms as they arise; no prior marketing experience is required. Begin with the
current consumer strategy rather than assuming a business model, social channel,
free offer, target market or named agent.

## Resolve the operating context

Reuse supplied context and resolve missing essentials:

- the business objective, audience/ICP and active offers;
- the operator's role, account and voice samples;
- the owner, drafting assistant and scheduler, if one is configured;
- approved channels, content/task stores and intake destinations;
- the consumer's voice, source/evidence and approval policies;
- the realistic time budget, publishing cadence and measurement baseline.

Relevant procedures and source documents must be available in the current
consumer's repository or connected storage. Do not pull another client's private
context or reproduce shared Core procedures in a local synchronized copy. Missing
credentials or a scheduler are setup gaps, not proof that the workflow is live.

## Who does what

- **Operator:** brings source material, reviews drafts, performs permitted
  account actions and logs relevant feedback.
- **Content owner:** decides strategy and approves content under their own name,
  following the consumer's approval rules.
- **Drafting assistant:** proposes posts, comments and replies with supporting
  sources and explains why each item belongs in the queue. It does not substitute
  for human judgment or invent lived experience.
- **Scheduling owner, if configured:** maintains already-authorized recurring
  jobs and reports failures. Do not create a schedule simply to finish onboarding.

External sending follows the user's rules. If the operator sends everything,
the assistant provides exact drafts and never calls a send action—even if a
casual request says "send"—unless the user explicitly changes that rule.

## First coached daily session

Run a small queue using current source material. Fit it to the agreed time
budget; the counts below are choices to make with the operator, not universal
quotas.

1. **Replies to recent activity.** Inspect comments on the operator's recent
   posts when access is available. Draft relevant replies, preserving context.
   Prioritize timely conversation without claiming a fixed algorithmic boost.
2. **Approval queue.** Present pending posts, comments or other permitted actions
   with a short reason, proposed text and evidence. The operator can edit or
   reject them. Capture useful voice feedback in the consumer's chosen location.
3. **Audience conversations.** Select a manageable number of relevant discussions
   using the configured ICP. Draft substantive comments where the operator has
   something useful to add; do not fill a volume quota with generic remarks.
4. **Inbound signals.** Record authorized, relevant inquiries in the configured
   pipeline with their source and date. Self-reported discovery can supplement
   analytics; do not claim it provides complete attribution. Capture personal
   contact details only in the approved private destination.

The assistant prepares the work, but the operator checks factual meaning,
relationship context and whether the draft sounds like them before acting.

## Establish a weekly routine

Agree a sustainable cadence for source collection, drafting, review, publishing
and measurement. An interview, annotated project artifact or written notes can
supply source material; do not require a recorded call when equivalent evidence
already exists. Obtain permission before recording or moving transcripts.

If several people publish, distinguish their content roles using actual expertise
and examples. Avoid duplicating posts. Every substantive claim needs appropriate
support: a source, direct experience, artifact or clearly labeled hypothesis.
A number or screenshot is useful when it proves the point, not a mandatory
prop in every post. Redact private material before preparing publishable assets.

A weekly review should compare actual cadence, audience responses, qualified
conversations and conversion progress against the consumer's own baseline and
goals. Explain missing measurement. Treat forecast milestones as assumptions,
not promised results after a fixed number of weeks.

## Practice prompts

Use the configured assistant in the chosen interface, for example:

- "Run today's queue using this approved source and my available time."
- "Show what needs my review, with the exact proposed text."
- "This reply doesn't sound like me; make it plainer."
- "Prepare a private pipeline entry for this inquiry and retain the source."
- "Draft a post from these notes without adding unsupported results."
- "What changed this month, and what evidence supports your recommendation?"

If a recommendation requires misleading claims, unsolicited bulk outreach or
unauthorized account access, stop that action and explain the issue. Do not solve
poor results by automatically increasing volume.

## Glossary

- **ICP:** the customer characteristics defined by the current strategy.
- **Content role:** the subject area and perspective a contributor owns.
- **Evidence:** a source or observation supporting a claim; proposed examples
  and invented numbers are not evidence.
- **Impressions:** reported appearances of content; not unique people or intent.
- **Engagement rate:** interactions divided by the stated denominator. Platforms
  may define it differently, so retain the metric definition.
- **Qualified conversation:** an exchange meeting the consumer's documented
  audience/problem criteria.
- **Pipeline:** the configured record of opportunities, with actual status.

## Onboarding completion

Complete one reviewed draft and one local/read-only queue walkthrough. Verify
that the operator can find source material, review text, identify the intended
account/destination and explain what remains unconfigured. Report any external
connection or publishing step that was not tested. Agree the next review point
without creating new recurring automation unless requested.
