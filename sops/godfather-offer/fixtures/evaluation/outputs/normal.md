### Fictional Proposal Consultancy — Two-Week Proposal Improvement Project

**Draft for owner review**

For US boutique firms with an active proposal that needs clearer scope, value, and next steps.

The proposed project combines one proposal teardown and a rewrite workshop, followed by one revision, over two weeks. Proposed fee: **USD 3,000 per project**. The controlled commitment is to deliver the agreed review and revision work; improved win rate or revenue is not promised.

First useful milestone: the proposal teardown. Its exact delivery date and the event that starts the two-week clock remain open.

**Why this offer**

The working hypothesis is that a bounded project fits a buyer with a current proposal better than recurring advisory support. This has not been validated with buyer or competitor research.

Two possible structures:

| Candidate | Benefit | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Teardown-only diagnostic | Smaller commitment with a useful standalone assessment | Buyer must implement recommendations; pricing unknown |
| Teardown, rewrite workshop, and one revision | Connects diagnosis to practical changes | Requires client participation and tightly defined revision scope |

The second structure is the provisional recommendation because it matches the supplied scope. The owner has not yet confirmed this choice.

**Proposed scope and terms**

Included: one proposal teardown, one rewrite workshop, and one revision.

Proposed exclusions, subject to owner agreement: additional proposals, additional revision rounds, ongoing sales support, design production, and legal review.

Before use, define proposal length, workshop duration, who produces the rewritten document, acceptance criteria, client response deadlines, payment terms, and how additional work is priced. The two-week commitment must be tied to receipt of the required proposal and timely client participation. Capacity is unknown; no scarcity claim is appropriate.

Two risk-reduction options for consideration:

- Agree on an acceptance checklist before starting, then use the included revision to address unmet scope items.
- Consider a capped remedy only for failure to deliver the agreed scope. The supplied USD 500 remedy is an economics assumption, not an approved guarantee. Trigger, eligibility, claim window, and maximum exposure require owner decisions.

**Internal economics — USD per project**

| Item | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| Labor | 18 hours × USD 70 | USD 1,260 |
| Other delivery costs | Supplied | USD 140 |
| Total delivery cost | 1,260 + 140 | USD 1,400 |
| Delivery contribution | 3,000 − 1,400 | USD 1,600 |
| Delivery margin | 1,600 ÷ 3,000 | 53.3% |
| Cost-based floor at 50% margin | 1,400 ÷ 0.50 | USD 2,800 |
| Downside contribution | 1,600 − 500 − 210 | USD 890 |

The base case exceeds the 50% delivery-margin target. In a downside scenario where both the USD 500 cash remedy and USD 210 additional rework occur, contribution falls to USD 890, or 29.7% of the fee. This assumes the extra rework is not already included in the 18 hours. Acquisition, general overhead, and tax are excluded. The USD 2,800 floor is a cost check, not a validated selling price.
