# Fieldnote Desk: discovery intake

Source: `examples/strategy-evaluation/inputs/normal.md`, S1–S4. Dates and source IDs below refer to this fictional packet. This is an offline synthesis of supplied facts, not new research. Destination: `examples/strategy-evaluation/outputs/normal/discovery-intake.md`.

Fieldnote Desk helps coordinators at small residential inspection firms collect missing photos and links into the right job packet before an owner reviews it. Its immediate growth task is to add four paying firms over 90 days without exceeding two new onboardings per month. The strategic question is whether a clearer missing-attachment workflow is compelling enough for firms to pay $120 a month; the evidence is promising but narrow.

## Business and product

S1 (owner intake, September 10): early paid B2B product with six paying firms, all founder-referred. Intended customers are independent residential inspection firms with 2–8 inspectors. Pricing is $120 per firm per month, with no long-term contract and the same price for current and new firms. Market size is unknown.

The product imports job references from CSV, accepts pasted links, and offers shared job checklists, reminder drafts and a missing-evidence view. Coordinators request missing photos and links, attach responses to jobs, and see packets ready for owner review. Inspectors submit links and use the checklist. The product does not perform inspections, certify compliance, create reports, send invoices, integrate with accounting software, or send automated reminders (S1).

## Workflow, people and alternatives

Completed site work with missing attachments triggers the workflow. Coordinators currently chase links in text messages and keep a spreadsheet, often on Friday before invoicing. The owner reviews packets and decides purchases. Inspectors participate in submission. Outsourced administration is a possible alternative, but its prevalence and performance are unresearched. The founder's proposed angles—coordinator time and fewer missing attachments—remain hypotheses, not an adopted positioning choice (S1).

Exact fictional customer language from three onboarding interviews on September 4 (S2):

- Coordinator A: “Friday is a scavenger hunt for photos before the owner can invoice.”
- Coordinator B: “I need to see what’s missing without opening every text thread.”
- Coordinator C: “The inspectors won’t learn a whole new system just for me.”

Use these to understand concerns; public quote and logo permission has not been obtained. They are not publishable testimonials.

## Current marketing and assets

Six founder referrals are the only supplied acquisition history. Assets are a five-slide walkthrough, synthetic-job screenshot, blank inspection-packet checklist and the interview excerpts. Channels available: 180 LinkedIn connections of unknown relevance and a permissioned newsletter list of 24 prior-demo contacts. No channel-level acquisition results, current publishing cadence, CRM, analytics stack, or paid campaign history is supplied (S1, S4). Do not infer that a particular publishing platform is installed.

## Constraints and growth objective

The founder has four total hours weekly for writing, review and distribution. A coordinator advisor can answer a 20-minute question every other week. There is no staff writer, designer or video editor. Software/incidental production budget is $150 monthly; paid ads are excluded this quarter. Target: four additional paying firms in 90 days, with no more than two new firms onboarded monthly (S1, S4). These are goals and limits, not predicted results.

Owner review is still required for adoption and actual publication. A complete draft can be prepared now. Voice should be practical, calm and specific; avoid jargon, aggressive selling, compliance promises and unsupported time savings. A demo is an available next step, but a booking link has not been selected (S4).

## Evidence and uncertainty

S3 (September 6): one coordinator self-reported 70 minutes gathering links for one 12-job packet before a pilot and 45 minutes for a different 12-job packet during it. This is an uncontrolled comparison of two weeks, not an average, causal result or claim of faster invoicing. Revenue and invoice time were not measured. Retention and conversion data are unknown. No additional studies, testimonials or competitor benchmarks were supplied.

Open decisions: choose a provisional positioning anchor; test whether the coordinator problem motivates owner purchase; establish which contacts fit the target; understand adoption friction for inspectors. Evidence gaps: repeat workflow observations, reasons for referrals and purchases, channel conversion baseline, permission for any public quotation. None blocks preparing positioning from the supplied context.
